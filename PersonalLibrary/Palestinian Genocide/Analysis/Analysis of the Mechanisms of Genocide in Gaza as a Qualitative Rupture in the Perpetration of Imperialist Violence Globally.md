# Abstract

The Accelerated Genocide, to borrow the term used by the Palestinian Feminist Collective, of Palestinians by the state of Israel bears a familiar visage to those of us who have borne witness to the imperialist violence of the US throughout West Asia and Africa. The images of apartment blocks, hospitals, and schools reduced to rubble atop their occupants, of parents and children alike robbed of each others' prescense, and of societies systematically torn apart physically, culturally, and logistically are burned into our collective consciousness like a brand. But despite all that we've seen, the Palestinian Genocide, specifically as it is being done in Gaza, has had an impact more deep and wide than any other imperialist campaigns in my lived history. Undoubtedly this is due in part to the unique position of Palestinians in relation to social media; I am unable to recall another genocide at any point in human history which was so widely publicized as it was being committed.

While the constant and global broadcast of Israeli violence is one of the unique factors of the Palestinian Genocide, it is not the factor which spurred this analysis on; rather, it is the automated factor, both of the killing and of the propaganda supporting it, which concerns and intrigues me. I am inclined to believe that we are bearing witness to the future of imperialist violence which is envisioned by the Western Capitalist Class, one in which the often violent repression of dissenters and occupied peoples is automated via AI and procedure like any other industry. 

For the purpose of this analysis I will investigate accounts and studies surrounding the American Genocide of Indigenous and Enslaved Peoples, the German Holocaust in Europe, and other documented genocides in order to compare and contrast the methods by which genocidal aggressors attempt to organize the mass killing and cultural erasure of their targeted out-groups. I will maintain a focus on the mechanisms by which genocide has historically been organized and on the dialectic evolution of the method of perpetration so as to understand the historical evolution and contemporary reality of genocide.
